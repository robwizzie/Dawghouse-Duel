      How Dawg House Duel works

      1. Two players. Each gets their own clock — 45 seconds is standard.
      2. One picture at a time. Only the player with control has a running clock.
      3. Name it right → the answer flashes up and control hands over. Your clock stops.
      4. Wrong → 3 seconds off your clock, same picture, still your turn.
      5. Stuck? Pass — the answer is revealed and you get a different picture, but it’s still your turn and it costs you 2 seconds.
      6. Only a right answer gets you off the clock. First to hit 00.0 loses.

      Three ways to play

      Duel — two of you at one screen, taking turns at the keyboard.

      Online Duel — two of you anywhere. You get a four-letter code; they open dawghouseduel.com/play, type the code, and their screen mirrors yours. They type their own answers on their own device. The answer key is never sent to them.

      Solo — one clock, and a score at the end.

      The rally

      The number above the picture is the streak of the player currently on the clock — their own consecutive correct answers, with no wrong and no pass. Each player keeps their own; you can’t break theirs and they can’t break yours.

      Two ways to answer

      Type it in — the game marks your answer. Nobody else needed. Spelling is forgiving, and short forms count: jokic, spongebob and doc ock all pass.

      Say it out loud — somebody else marks it. This is the one for filming: put the laptop under the camera, and whoever’s marking uses the Host View on their phone or a second window. It shows the answer, the alternates that count, and big correct / wrong / pass buttons.

      Solo

      One clock, one player. Keep going until it runs out and see how many you got. Your longest streak is on the score card.

      Picture modes

      Silhouette blacks the picture out and fills it in after a few seconds. Extreme zoom starts far too close and pulls back. Blurred sharpens up. On a correct answer or a pass you always see the real picture.
